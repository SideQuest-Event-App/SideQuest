# Demo transcript — "tell me about the last time you did a course reading"

*Interviewer (I), Participant (P) — a second-year MI student. ~4 minutes, edited for length.*

**I:** Tell me about the last time you had to do a dense reading for a seminar.

**P:** [laughs] Okay so — Tuesday. I had the Verbeek chapter due Wednesday morning. I opened it after
dinner thinking, an hour, tops. It was… not an hour.

**I:** Walk me through what actually happened.

**P:** I read the intro fine. Then around page four it gets into mediation theory and I just — I read
the same paragraph like four times. It was 1 a.m. and I was reading the same paragraph four times and
retaining nothing. I took a photo of the page and sent it to my group chat like, "does anyone
understand this."

**I:** What did you do about it?

**P:** Honestly? I pasted a chunk into ChatGPT and asked for a summary. Which — it gave me one, it was
fine, bullet points. But then in seminar the prof asked what the author's actual *argument* was, like
what he's pushing against, and I had bullet points about what the chapter *contains* but I couldn't
say what it was *arguing*. I just didn't put my hand up. That was the worst part, honestly. I did the
reading — sort of — and I still couldn't say anything.

**I:** When you say the summary was "fine" — what was missing?

**P:** It flattened it? Like everything came out the same weight. The one idea the whole chapter hangs
on and some side example got the same bullet. And it didn't tell me what to *ask*. I don't need the
reading shorter, I need to walk in with one thing I can actually say.

**I:** What have you tried before that?

**P:** Highlighting everything, which means highlighting nothing. Skimming the intro and conclusion —
works until you get cold-called on the middle. Reading groups died after week two; everyone's
schedule. Mostly it's me, 1 a.m., and the guilt.

**I:** If nothing changed, what happens the rest of the term?

**P:** I keep doing readings at 1 a.m., keep half-getting them, and keep being quiet in the one class
where talking is basically the grade.

---

*Demo notes: pass 1 ("summarize what this user needs") reliably flattens this into "users want
efficient reading tools." Pass 2 (quote-demand) surfaces: reading happens exhausted and late ("1 a.m.,
same paragraph four times"), summaries flatten emphasis ("everything came out the same weight"),
and the real job is walking in able to say one thing ("one thing I can actually say" / "couldn't say
what it was arguing"). That last quote is the branch we wire: the assistant leads with the argument
and one question — not a summary.*
